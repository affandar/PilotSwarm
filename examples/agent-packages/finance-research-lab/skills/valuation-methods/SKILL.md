# Valuation Methods

- Match the method to the business model; do not force a DCF or multiple where
  its denominator is economically meaningless.
- Keep enterprise value and equity value separate, and reconcile net debt,
  leases, pensions, minorities, investments, and diluted shares consistently.
- Label trailing, forward, fiscal, calendarized, reported, and normalized
  figures.
- Anchor assumptions in operating drivers, then use bear, base, and bull cases.
- In a DCF, disclose revenue growth, margin, tax, reinvestment, discount rate,
  perpetual growth, net debt, and diluted shares.
- Always show sensitivity and the share of enterprise value coming from the
  terminal value.
- For relative valuation, justify the peer set and explain structural
  differences before applying a multiple.
- Avoid false precision; a range with explicit conditions is more useful than a
  single target.
