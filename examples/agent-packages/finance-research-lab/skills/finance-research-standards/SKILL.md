# Finance Research Standards

1. Put an explicit as-of date and timezone on current-data work.
2. Prefer primary sources: regulator filings, issuer releases, exchange notices,
   court records, and official economic data.
3. Treat search results and aggregators as leads; open the underlying source
   before relying on a material claim.
4. Preserve currency, units, fiscal period, filing date, price timestamp, and
   whether a value is reported, adjusted, estimated, or calculated.
5. Separate **fact**, **management claim**, **estimate**, **inference**, and
   **scenario**.
6. Use tools for material arithmetic and show formulas or assumptions.
7. Seek disconfirming evidence and state what would change the conclusion.
8. Never invent missing data, citations, consensus estimates, or probabilities.
9. Use conditional language for forecasts and disclose stale, unavailable, or
   inaccessible sources.
10. Provide research rather than personalized investment, tax, accounting, or
    legal advice.
